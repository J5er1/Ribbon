The promise

You will never be reading alone, and you will never be behind.

The competition is not other Bible apps. It’s that reading Scripture with someone you love currently happens badly — over text message, out of sync, and with a low hum of guilt about the days you missed.

So Ribbon has no plan, no schedule and no pace. Nothing in it counts. It is a room, not a program: warm, dim, unhurried, and occupied by someone else.

One · Presence

You can see who else is reading, while you read.

A soft form comes partway out of the right edge, carrying their face. Not a cursor, not a dot on a map — a person, half in the room with you.

Nobody here
The form is absent. Not greyed, not a placeholder, not “no one is reading.” Absence is the honest rendering of absence.
Following
Tap a portrait and you go where they are. A hairline runs down the right edge while your scroll is theirs. Any scroll of your own gives it back — no dialog, no asking.
Being followed
Visible, but small. Their portrait tucks against yours and one line reads Ruth is with you. There is no count of followers, in either direction.
Reading quietly
You see everyone. Nobody sees you. Your own form stays on screen as a small closed shape so you never forget you’re invisible.
Thinking of you
Press and hold their portrait. It fills with your ink. They get one soft tap on the shoulder, carrying nothing but your name — no message, no emoji, no count of how many.

Hold a verse. Write, or speak — press and hold, and the waveform draws in your ink as you talk. The note stays where you left it. Always left, never posted, shared or sent: being found later is the whole point.

Notes open in place. The line height opens and the note unfurls beneath the verse. Never a sheet — a sheet takes you out of the room, and this is the one moment where staying in it matters most.

catching

Banking is an act. It is never on the way to anywhere.

A fire reports a state, never a count.

No numbers. No fractions. No percentages. No “six of twelve,” no day tallies, nowhere, ever. A countable goal is an accusation: it tells you exactly how much is missing and whose absence it was. An abstract one can only report a mood.

Its size is set by the book and never changes while you read it. What grows underneath is the bed of coals — because nobody can look at a bed of coals and work out how much is left. Where you’ve got to is shown by the text, and nowhere else.

Philemon is a single sitting. Isaiah is a winter. The fire is that size from the first verse — so the length of what you’ve taken on is something you feel rather than something you’re told. Below, the three scales at the ratio the app uses.

What you keep

The flame goes down. The light stays.

Finish a book and its fire settles into an ember. The shelf is every book you’ve read together, each one the size its fire was, sitting on a baseline that isn’t drawn — the light on the surface is what implies the surface.

Open one and the whole record is there: every note left, every highlight in its ink, the dates, and who read it. Nothing about it is a score, and nothing about it can be sorted — a shelf you can sort is a database.

One day this is a book.

The app

Six destinations, one root, and no tab bar anywhere.

The app opens in the room and stays there: the fire, who’s here, and one way in. Everything else is a push off the room or a sheet over it — six places in the whole product, not fifteen. Three of them are below.

No tab bar, because a tab bar is a claim that the app has several equal purposes. Reading has no top bar, no back button and no toolbar until you ask for one. There are two ways out and both are at the bottom.

For groups and ministries

A leader gets the same room as everyone else.

You make rooms and invite people into them. That’s the whole of the leader’s tooling. A room holds six; start another for the rest.

No roster
There is no dashboard and no attendance view. That would be a count of the most sensitive kind there is.
Same view
A leader sees their own rooms exactly as any member does — the fire, who’s here, the way in.
Quiet billing
Billing is per leader, and members never see it.
Under 13
A room with anyone under 13 needs an adult member, and voice notes aren’t transcribed.

Pricing

The first book is free, all the way through.

Not seven days. A trial makes the clock the thing you’re aware of, and a clock is a count. You read a whole book together, you keep the ember, and only then is there anything to decide.

One table. No monthly-or-annual theatre, and nothing marked “most popular.”
Your first book Free — start to finish, with the ember at the end.
A room of two About $8 a month. One person pays. The other never sees a paywall and never learns who pays.
A room of three to six A flat monthly for the room, priced above the pair. Still one payer.
Youth ministry Per leader, invoiced.

If a room lapses

It pauses. Presence stops and new notes stop. Reading, every note already left, every highlight and the whole shelf stay — and Scripture is never locked, by anyone, for any reason. You can start the room again at any time.

Read it together.

Two ribbons in one book: two people, one text, different places, still bound together. That’s the whole idea, and it’s where the name came from.
