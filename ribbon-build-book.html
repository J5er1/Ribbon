    The Ribbon build book.

    Every screen, every state, every gesture — and the reasoning underneath each one, so the next person to touch this can tell an intention from an accident.

    00How to read this

    What this document decides, what it inherits, and what it deliberately leaves open.

    The brand brief settled the name, the positioning, the voice, and the never-ship list. Since it was written, four things changed and this document follows the newer decisions: the wordmark is Cesso, not Fraunces; the palette is chartreuse on true black with ivory, not crimson and lamp; the mark is the Wave; the app icon is the Cesso R. with the ribbon living inside the app. Where the brief and this document disagree on those four, this document wins. Everywhere else — especially the never-ship list and the product vocabulary — the brief still governs.

    Three kinds of statement appear here, and they are marked differently on purpose:

    Law
    A rule that generates other decisions. Breaking one doesn't cost a screen, it costs the product. Marked with a chartreuse rule down the left.
    Decision
    A call made here that could reasonably have gone another way. Stated plainly, with the reasoning attached so it can be argued with later.
    Open
    Genuinely unresolved. Collected in §16 rather than quietly settled. If you find me deciding one of these in passing, that's a bug in the document.

    Screens carry stable codes (S01–S26). Use them in tickets, in Figma frame names, in commit messages. They will outlive this document's section numbering.

    01The product, in one page

    Everything below is downstream of these six sentences. When a decision feels arbitrary, come back here.

    What it is. Ribbon is an app for reading the Bible with one person, or a few — a partner on the same couch, or a friend four time zones away. It does three things: it lets you see who else is reading while you read, it holds the notes you leave each other in the margin, and it keeps one warm shared object alive instead of a streak.

    Who it's for, in order: couples, then friend groups and small studies of three to six, then youth leaders running a group.

    The promise. You will never be reading alone, and you will never be behind.

    The feeling. A room, not a program. Warm, dim, unhurried, and occupied by someone else.

    Law 1 · The promise generates the product“You will never be behind” is not marketing. It forbids reading plans, schedules, pace, catch-up, and every number that could be read as a shortfall. Any feature that could make a person feel late has already failed, no matter how well it tests.
    Law 2 · A fire reports a state, never a countNo counts, fractions, percentages, day tallies, or progress bars anywhere near a handiwork — in the app, in a widget, in a notification, in a screenshot, in the App Store listing. A countable goal is an accusation; it tells you exactly how much is missing and whose absence it was.
    The distinction that keeps Law 2 usableAddresses are not scores. “Mark 4:9” is an address. “Chapter 6” is an address. “6 of 16 chapters,” “68%,” “12 days,” “3 books this year” are scores. Verse numbers, chapter numbers, and dates are all fine and always have been — the brief sets verse numbers in small caps precisely so they can exist without shouting. What is forbidden is any figure that answers how much is left or how often did you. When in doubt, ask whether a person could feel accused by the number. If yes, cut it.

    What it is not. Not a habit tracker with a cross on it. Not a study platform. Not a social network — no feed, no follower count, no strangers, no discovery. It has no opinion about anyone's theology and no interest in their consistency.

    Law 3 · The hesitant partner must be able to install thisSomeone who is unsure about the Bible has to be able to put Ribbon on their home screen without it being a statement, and open it without feeling recruited. This governs the icon, the App Store screenshots, the onboarding copy, the notification text on a lock screen, and every default that could be read as a nudge toward piety.

    02What this document decides

    Nine structural calls, made here, with the reasoning attached. Argue with the reasoning, not the conclusion.

    2.1  There is no tab bar

    The room is the app. Everything else is a push or a sheet off it. A tab bar would put four permanent competing destinations at the bottom of a product whose entire emotional claim is that it is one warm place — and it would hand iOS a glass slab in the most conspicuous position on the screen. Rooms are switched from the room's own name, not from a tab.

    This is a real cost: no tab bar means discoverability of the shelf and settings rests on scroll and on one sheet. Accepted. Ribbon has six destinations, not fifteen.

    2.2  There is no reading plan, no schedule, and no pace

    Direct consequence of Law 1. You read where you read. The handiwork responds to the room's reading, not to a calendar. Two people who want to stay together do it by following each other (§4.2), not by a plan telling them both where to be on Tuesday.

    This is the single largest departure from every competitor, and it is also the largest product risk: plans are how most people think Bible apps work, and some will bounce off the absence. The answer to “where do we start?” is a good book chooser (S13), not a plan.

    2.3  The group is called a room

    The brief's emotional target — “a room, not a program” — hands us the noun. It is already on the approved word list, it is warm, it is not churchy, and it survives being said out loud by a hesitant partner (“I'm in a room with Ruth” is fine; “I'm in a Bible study group” is a commitment). Alternatives considered: table (too Eucharistic), circle (too therapy), company (too corporate). Logged as an open question anyway — see §16 — because it is a hard word to change later.

    2.4  A person can be in several rooms, and rooms don't know about each other

    A couple's room and a Thursday study are different rooms with different handiworks, different shelves, different ink assignments, and separate notification settings. Nothing crosses between them. Notes you leave your wife are not visible to your small group, and there is no aggregate “your reading” view anywhere in the product — that view would be a score.

    2.5  Scripture is never locked

    Not by a lapsed subscription, not by being offline, not by a room going quiet. If a subscription ends, the room is paused — presence stops and new notes can't be left — but every word of Scripture and every note already left stays readable forever. See §14.3.

    2.6  Translation is a personal setting, not a room setting

    You read the Berean, Ruth reads the WEB, and her note still lands on verse 9 because notes pin to verse addresses, not to text offsets. Both launch translations share versification, so this is nearly free at launch and becomes a real engineering constraint the day a differently-versified translation is added. Better to design for it now.

    The detail that makes it feel authored: when Ruth's note quotes the verse, it renders in Ruth's translation, in small type, so you see the words she was looking at.

    2.7  Presence is faces, not cursors

    The app is touch-first, so there is no cursor to share and a text caret would be a lie. Presence is a portrait, half-emerged from the right edge of the screen, and its whole job is to say someone is here before it says anything else.

    2.8  The campfire ships first, and it is one of several

    The handiwork is the general mechanic: a single shared object a room completes together. The campfire ships first because it is abstract, and losing an abstract thing hurts less than losing something countable like a pane of glass or a section of a painting. Mosaic, stained glass, and the painting follow once the fire has proven the mechanic. The code should say handiwork everywhere and fire only in the campfire's own module.

    The test every future handiwork has to pass: it may not be assembled from discrete countable units. A mosaic of tiles or a window of panes fails Law 2 the moment somebody counts the empty ones — so a mosaic has to spread as a field of colour rather than arrive tile by tile, and a window has to deepen rather than fill. If a handiwork can't be drawn that way, it doesn't ship, however good the idea sounded.

    2.9  One handiwork per book, sized by the book

    Philemon's fire is small. Isaiah's is large. Size is set once from the book's word count and never changes during the read — because a growing size would be a progress bar wearing a costume. What accumulates instead is the bed of coals (§4.1).

    04The mechanics

    Nine systems. Each one is described here once, in full, and referenced by every screen that touches it.

    4.1  The handiwork, and the fire

    A handiwork is one shared object per book, kept alive by everyone in the room. The campfire is the first kind. It has exactly four states and no numbers.

    StateMeansReads asEntered when
    CatchingJust started, or just restarted after a long quietSmall, low, a few licks, working at itThe reading opens, or fuel arrives after a long quiet. It is also where a fire that subsides on its own comes to rest
    BurningSomeone has been readingFull, active, irregular flickerFuel within the last ~36 hours from any one person
    SteadyThe room has been reading togetherBroad, even, a wide warm throw, almost calmFuel from two or more people within the same ~36-hour window
    BankedDeliberately kept low and warmCoals under ash, a dim glow, no flameOnly when someone marks a quiet day. Banked is an act, never a lapse

    Fuel, air, and subsiding

    • Fuel is reading. Any member reading any passage feeds the fire. It never matters who, or where in the book.
    • Air is overlap. Two or more people feeding within the same window is what lifts burning to steady. This is the only place in the product where togetherness is mechanically rewarded, and the return is a mood, not a bonus.
    • Subsiding is time. Without fuel the fire eases down through the states over days. It never goes out, it never resets, and it never says why. Its floor is catching, never banked — a fire nobody tended is not the same object as a fire somebody banked, and drawing them alike would turn an act of care into the look of neglect.
    Law 4 · The coal bed remembers, the flame reports the momentThe fire's size is fixed by the book at the start and never changes. Its state is recent activity only. What accumulates over a long read is the bed of coals beneath — deepening, throwing a wider warm light. Coals are the one thing that may grow, because nobody can look at a bed of coals and compute how much is left. Progress through the book is shown by the text itself and nowhere else.
    Why the thresholds are vague on purpose“~36 hours” is deliberately not a day. A 24-hour boundary manufactures a midnight deadline, and a deadline is a count you can be late for. A 36-hour window means someone who reads at 10 p.m. Monday and 9 a.m. Wednesday never experiences a lapse, and nobody in the room ever knows there was a threshold at all. Never surface the window in copy, in a tooltip, or in support docs.

    The fire at three scales

    Size is derived from word count at the moment a reading opens, mapped into three drawn sizes rather than a continuous scale — a continuous scale invites comparison, three sizes read as a fact about the book.

    ScaleRoughlyExamplesFrame height on the room screen
    SmallUnder ~5,000 wordsPhilemon, Jude, 2 John, Obadiah~120 pt
    Medium~5,000–20,000Philippians, Ruth, James, Mark~180 pt
    LargeOver ~20,000Isaiah, Psalms, Genesis, Jeremiah~240 pt

    4.2  Presence

    The form emerges partway from the right edge of the reading screen. Left edge is the gutter and belongs to notes; right edge belongs to people. That split governs the reading surface, on every platform and at every width; elsewhere in the app portraits sit where the layout wants them.

    The form's five states

    Nobody here
    The form is absent. Not greyed, not a placeholder, not “no one is reading.” Absence is the honest rendering of absence, and a placeholder would be a small daily reminder that you are alone.
    Someone here
    A soft lozenge, roughly 44 × 64 pt, about half off-screen, carrying their portrait. It eases out over 400 ms with a soft haptic when they arrive.
    Several here
    The lozenge becomes a short vertical stack you can flick through — one person at a time in the frame, with a hairline of the next one behind. Never a row of shrinking avatars.
    Following
    The followed person's portrait takes a thin ring in their ink, and a hairline chartreuse thread runs down the screen's right edge. Your scroll is theirs.
    Reading quietly
    You have made yourself invisible. Others see nothing at all; you see a small closed shape at the edge so you never forget. It stays tappable even when you are alone — otherwise a person whose partner logs off is stranded invisible with no way back to the toggle.

    Following, and leaving

    • Tap a portrait to follow. If they are in Mark 6 and you are in Mark 2, you go to Mark 6 — with a page-fly transition, no confirmation dialog, because a dialog here would make joining someone feel like a commitment.
    • Any scroll of your own breaks the follow, with a gentle rubber-band on the first gesture so it never happens by accident. No modal, no “stop following?” — you just have your own scroll back.
    • Getting back. After a follow ends, the form quietly offers back to where you were for about two minutes, then forgets.
    • Being followed is visible but small: their portrait tucks against yours, and one line of small caps reads Ruth is with you. No count of followers, ever.

    Reading quietly

    A per-session toggle in the expanded panel: read quietly. You see everyone; nobody sees you. Your own form stays on screen as a small closed shape so you never forget you're invisible — the alternative is a person who thinks they're present for weeks and isn't.

    This exists for Law 3. A hesitant partner needs to be able to open the book without an audience, and “I can slip in without being watched” is what makes the first week survivable.

    Idle and away

    After roughly four minutes with no scroll, a present person's portrait dims by about 40% and the form's edge softens — here, but still. They are not removed. Backgrounding the app removes them after a short grace so a glance at a text message doesn't read as leaving.

    4.3  Thinking of you

    The most loaded feature in the product, and it must be nearly invisible. From the expanded presence panel, press and hold a person's portrait: it fills with your ink over about 700 ms; release completes it. They get a single soft haptic and a notification carrying nothing but your name.

    • No message, no emoji, no reaction picker. The whole meaning is that it is contentless.
    • No count of taps sent or received, in either direction.
    • Repeats collapse. Several taps inside a few minutes become one delivery, so it can't be spammed and there's no rate-limit message to write.
    • Haptic on the receiving end is a single soft transient with a short decay — the brief's “tap on the shoulder.” It is the only thing in the app permitted to feel fast.

    4.4  Notes left

    A note is a voice memo or a written thought pinned to a verse. Always left, never posted, shared, or sent.

    Marks in the gutter

    Voice
    A solid dot, 6 pt, in the author's ink.
    Written
    An open ring, 6 pt, 1.4 pt stroke, in the author's ink.
    Unfound
    The mark breathes — opacity 0.65 → 1.0 over 4 s, eased both ways. Slow enough that it never reads as an alert.
    Found
    Steady at 55% opacity. It does not disappear; the record stays.
    Yours
    Same shapes, never breathing. You do not need to be told about your own note.
    Stacked
    Up to three marks stack vertically on one verse; beyond that, three marks plus a small centred dot triplet. Tapping opens the whole stack.
    Pending
    Composed offline: the mark appears immediately as a hairline outline in your ink, and fills in when it lands. No spinner, no toast, no retry button.

    Opening a note

    Tapping a mark expands the note inline. The line height opens and the note unfurls beneath the verse, indented to the measure, the author's portrait small at the left. Never a modal, never a bottom sheet — a sheet takes you out of the room and this is the one moment where staying in it matters most.

    A voice note draws a thin waveform in the author's ink and plays in place. Beneath it, the transcript, muted, collapsed by default, expandable. Transcripts are not optional: they are how the deaf read this app, and how anyone finds a note again six months later.

    Leaving a note

    1. Long-press a verse. It lifts about 2 pt with a soft shadow — the verse becomes a physical thing you're holding.
    2. A small toolbar rises at the bottom: highlight (with the ink swatches), write, speak.
    3. Speak is press-and-hold. The waveform draws in your ink as you talk. Release to keep; drag away to discard, with the waveform receding rather than a confirmation.
    4. Write opens a composer sized to the note, growing as you type. No formatting toolbar. No title field.

    4.5  Ink

    Ink is a highlight color. Its meaning changes with the size of the room, and the transition between the two meanings is a moment worth designing.

    RoomHow ink worksWhy
    Two peopleBoth draw from the whole palette freely, choosing per highlightColor is expressive — this one's a promise, this one's a question. With two people you always know who made a mark, so color is free to mean something else.
    Three or moreEach person picks one ink; every highlight they make is that colorColor becomes identity, because with five people in a chapter you can no longer tell whose mark is whose by elimination.

    The transition

    When a third person joins, the two existing members are asked to pick their ink — as an invitation, not an interruption, sitting on the room screen until answered. Existing highlights are never recolored. One line of small caps under them explains it:

    These keep their colors. They're from when the room was two.

    If the room drops back to two, ink stays assigned. Nothing is taken away, and the free palette returns only if someone asks for it in settings.

    Rendering

    A highlight is a translucent wash at ~24%, bleeding about 2 pt past the glyph box, with slightly irregular top and bottom edges so it reads as ink soaking into paper rather than a filled rectangle. Overlapping highlights blend — multiply, not stack — so two people marking the same verse produces a third color, which is the correct emotional result.

    The eight inks are the brief's, unchanged. Chartreuse is not among them and never becomes selectable: it is the brand's, not the user's.

    4.6  The cards

    Reflection questions that open once everyone has answered. They sit at the end of a chapter, below the last verse.

    Sealed
    The question is visible. Your answer field is open. One line below: This opens when everyone has answered. Never “Ruth hasn't answered yet” — naming the person who hasn't turns the card into an accusation.
    Answered
    Your answer is saved and visible only to you. You can edit it right up until the card opens.
    Opening
    When the last answer lands, the card turns over slowly — 480 ms, ease-out, no bounce — and everyone's answers are there together. The room gets one notification: The cards are open.
    Set down
    Any member can retire a sealed card for the room. It leaves without ceremony and doesn't come back. This is the pressure valve that lets a card wait forever without becoming a debt.
    Never
    A sealed card has no expiry, no reminder after N days, no “nudge Ruth” button. It waits. Waiting is the feature.

    Questions are written by Ribbon, one or two per chapter, in the brief's voice: short, concrete, second person, answerable in a sentence. “What did you notice that the other one probably didn't?” works. “How is God calling you to grow?” is exactly the register to avoid. Authorship at scale is an open question — §16.

    4.7  Quiet days

    The grace mechanic. Marked by a person, never detected by the app.

    • Marking a quiet day banks the fire for the room and pauses subsiding.
    • The room sees who did it: Ruth banked the fire. It is an act of care, performed in public — not an excuse filed in private.
    • Available from the room screen and from a notification's long-press actions. Never surfaced after a lapse, which would make it an apology.
    • No limit, no ledger, no “quiet days remaining.” A counted grace is not grace.

    4.8  The shelf, and embers

    Finishing a book turns its handiwork into an ember. The shelf is every ember the room has, in a row, each the size its fire was.

    Answering the brief's open question: the shelf does not draw a shelf. The embers sit on a shared baseline with a faint warm bloom beneath them — light on a surface implies the surface. If that proves too subtle in testing, the fallback is a single hairline rule at 12% opacity, and nothing heavier.

    Tapping an ember opens that reading's record (S11): the book, the date range, every note left, every highlight, every card the room opened. Immutable. This is what the printed keepsake is made from.

    4.9  Time, days, and zones

    A couple four time zones apart must never be told they are a day behind because of the date line.

    • Subsiding runs on elapsed hours since the room's last fuel, not on calendar days. There is no midnight, anywhere, for anyone.
    • A quiet day is marked in the marker's local day, and displayed to everyone else as “today” or “yesterday” relative to their clock.
    • Timestamps are relative and coarse — this morning, last night, Tuesday — never “14 hours ago,” which is a count, and never a wall-clock time that would reveal that someone was awake at 3 a.m.
    • Date ranges on an ember record are absolute (March – June) because they are addresses in time, not durations.

    05Navigation

    Six destinations, one root, no tabs. Everything is a push off the room or a sheet over it.

    The rules

    • Cold start goes to the room you were last in. No splash screen, no “welcome back,” no interstitial. The app opening on the room is the app saying nothing, which is correct.
    • The only chrome on the room screen is the room's name, top-left, in small caps. Tapping it opens the rooms sheet. That is the entire navigation bar.
    • The reading screen has no top bar and no back button. Two ways out, both at the bottom: the Wave mark, and a downward drag from scroll-top that settles like a book closing.
    • Sheets are for things you finish and dismiss — the composer, settings, the rooms list, the book chooser. Pushes are for places you go.
    • Nothing in the app opens a modal alert except destructive confirmations (leaving a room, deleting an account) and system permission prompts.

    06Workflows

    Twelve journeys, end to end, including the ones that go wrong.

    6.1  First run to first read

    The whole thing is four decisions and no tour. There is no carousel, no feature walkthrough, and no stack of permission prompts at launch.

    1. Open. The Wave mark, and one line: Read it together. It holds for about 900 ms and then dissolves into the first question. This is the only branded moment in the product.
    2. “Who's reading with you?” Two answers: Start a room and I have an invite. No third option, no sign-in-first wall — account creation happens inside whichever path you pick.
    3. Your name and portrait. The portrait is asked for with a reason attached — They'll see your face when you're reading. — because that is true and it is the only reason that would persuade someone. Skipping gives a monogram in your ink, and the app never asks again.
    4. Invite them. A link, into the system share sheet. Copy: Send this to the person you're reading with. No contact-list permission. No “find friends.” No suggested people.
    5. Pick a book, and start. You can read alone immediately while the invite is out. The fire catches with one person. The presence form is simply absent, and nothing comments on that.

    The one-time hint

    At the top of your first passage, one line in small caps that never appears again:

    Notes go in the margin. Hold a verse to leave one.

    When permissions get asked

    • Notifications: after the first note is left or found — never at launch. In context: Tell you when Ruth leaves a note?
    • Microphone: the first time press-and-hold-to-speak is used.
    • Camera / photos: only if the portrait picker is opened.

    6.2  The daily read — the core loop

    The loop this product lives or dies on. It should take one tap to enter and never require a decision.

    1. Open the app → the room. The fire is the first thing on screen, in whatever state it's in.
    2. One control: Continue in Mark. Tap.
    3. Reading opens at your position, not the room's. The presence form is out if anyone is there.
    4. You read. Marks in the gutter show what was left while you were gone.
    5. End of chapter → the passage end (S03): the cards if there are any, Mark 7, and the way out.
    6. Close the book. The room again — the fire has moved, quietly, without announcing it.
    The thing to protect in this loopNothing may be inserted between opening the app and reading. No “what's new,” no streak card, no verse of the day, no re-engagement prompt, no rating request positioned at the moment of completion. The fastest path from launch to Scripture is the product.

    6.3  Leaving a note, and finding one

    Two halves of one workflow, days apart. The asymmetry is the point.

    Leaving

    1. Long-press a verse; it lifts.
    2. Toolbar: highlight, write, speak.
    3. Compose. Voice draws its waveform live.
    4. Release or save. The mark appears in the gutter immediately.
    5. No confirmation. No “note left!” toast. It's in the margin; you can see it.

    Finding

    1. A notification, or nothing at all.
    2. The room lists it: Ruth left you a note at Mark 4:9.
    3. Tap → reading opens at that verse, the mark breathing.
    4. Tap the mark → the note unfurls in place.
    5. The mark settles to found. Ruth is not told you read it.
    DecisionThere are no read receipts. A read receipt turns a gift into a transaction and makes the un-found note into an unanswered message. The author sees only that the note exists.

    6.4  Opening the cards

    1. You reach the end of a chapter. A sealed card sits below the last verse.
    2. You answer. It saves. The card stays sealed. You may edit until it opens.
    3. Days pass. Nothing nags — no reminder, no badge, no “Ruth is waiting.”
    4. The last answer lands. The card turns over, slowly, and everyone gets one notification: The cards are open.
    5. Open cards live on in the ember record forever.

    Escape hatch: anyone may set down a sealed card, for the room, at any time. It leaves quietly.

    6.5  Finishing a book

    The one place in the product that gets to feel like an event — and it still has no confetti, no badge, and no number.

    1. The last verse of the last chapter. Below it, more space than usual.
    2. The fire, drawn large, one last time — and then it settles into an ember over about 2.5 s. The flame goes down; the light stays.
    3. One line, in Cesso: Mark. Below it, the date range in small caps.
    4. Two ways on: Put it on the shelf, and Start another.
    5. Everyone in the room is notified — this is the one notification that goes to people who weren't there.
    You finished Mark together.

    Not: You've completed your 4th book! 🔥 12-day streak!

    6.6  Starting a new fire

    The book chooser (S13) is the most conventional screen in the app and should stay that way — this is navigation, not atmosphere.

    • Books in canonical order, grouped, with a quiet search field that matches references and text.
    • Each book shows its fire size, drawn, as the honest way of saying how long it is. No word counts, no reading-time estimates — “Isaiah, about 14 hours” is a commitment you can fail.
    • A short list at the top: Good places to start together — Mark, Ruth, Philippians, John, Psalms. Editorial, not algorithmic.
    • Books already on the shelf are marked with their ember and can be read again; a second reading gets its own fire and its own ember.

    6.7  A third person joins

    1. An existing member invites someone. Any member can invite — a room isn't owned.
    2. They accept. The room is now three.
    3. The two originals get an invitation on the room screen to pick an ink. Not a blocking dialog; it waits.
    4. The newcomer picks from what's left during their onboarding.
    5. Existing highlights keep their colors, with the one-line explanation.
    6. The handiwork is untouched. The fire doesn't reset, and the newcomer doesn't “start behind” — there is nothing to be behind.

    Six is the ceiling. At six, invite is disabled with a plain reason: A room holds six. Start another for the rest. The ceiling exists because presence stops meaning anything past six faces and because eight inks minus brand reserve leaves no room for growth.

    6.8  Someone leaves, or a room ends

    The hardest workflow in the product, because a couples app has to handle a breakup with dignity.

    Leaving
    A member can leave any room. One confirmation, plainly worded, no guilt: Leave this room? You'll keep the books on your shelf.
    Their notes
    One question at the moment of leaving, defaulting to leave them: Leave your notes behind? They were left for the other person. Taking them back should be possible and should never be the default.
    Their highlights
    Stay, always. A highlight is a mark on a shared page, not a possession.
    The room after
    Keeps its shelf and its fire. If one person remains, it becomes a room of one and reading continues. It is never auto-deleted, and it is never renamed to something sad.
    Closing a room
    Requires everyone to agree, or 30 days of silence plus one member's request. On close, every member gets an export of the whole shelf — notes, highlights, cards — as a readable file. No dark pattern, no retention offer, no “are you sure? you'll lose everything.”
    Account deletion
    Removes the person everywhere. Notes left follow the same “leave them behind?” question, asked once, at deletion.

    6.9  Subscribing, lapsing, restoring

    See §14 for the model. The workflows:

    • Subscribing happens from the shelf after the first book is finished, or from settings at any time. One person pays; the room is entitled. The other person never sees a paywall, ever, and never learns whether they are the payer.
    • Lapsing: the room is paused — presence off, new notes off. Reading, existing notes, highlights and the shelf all stay. The copy names no one: The room is paused. It can be started again any time.
    • Restoring is one tap and everything returns, including the fire in the state it was in. Nothing was deleted.
    • Payment failure gets a grace window before quiet, and the message states what did not happen: The card was declined. Nothing changed in your room.

    6.10  New device, lost account, offline

    • Sign-in is a passkey where available, an emailed code otherwise. No passwords. No “sign in with” buttons that leak the app's name into a third-party account list — that matters for Law 3.
    • New device: sign in, rooms restore, books re-download on Wi-Fi in the background. The shelf is available immediately; text follows.
    • Offline reading is a first-class case. Downloaded books read normally. The presence form is absent. Notes queue with hairline marks. There is no offline banner, because the app being usable is not news.
    • Lost access entirely: email recovery, and the room's other members can re-invite you into the same membership so your ink and your notes reattach rather than duplicating.

    6.11  Youth ministry and groups

    • A leader creates rooms and invites into them; there is no roster, no dashboard, and no attendance view — that would be a count of the most sensitive kind.
    • A leader sees their own rooms exactly as any member does.
    • Billing is per-leader for N rooms, and members never see it.
    • Minors: a room containing anyone under 13 disables voice notes' transcription upload and requires an adult member. This needs legal review before launch — flagged in §16.

    6.12  What Ribbon never does

    • Never notifies anyone about someone's absence, inactivity, or lapse.
    • Never shows a person their own consistency, in any form.
    • Never compares two people in a room.
    • Never asks for a rating at a moment of emotional completion.
    • Never sends a re-engagement notification. If you stop opening it, it stops speaking.
    • Never puts a numeric badge on the app icon.

    9.2  Texture

    A tileable paper grain sits over the ground at 3.5% opacity in dark, 2.8% in light. This matters more than usual: at #0B0B0A a flat fill reads as switched-off app chrome, and the grain is the entire difference between a near-black screen and an unlit room. Ship it as a 140 px tile, applied above the ground and below all content, never over Scripture glyphs themselves.

    9.3  Haptics

    • Someone arrives: one soft transient, low intensity.
    • Verse lifts on long-press: one soft transient, the moment it lifts, not on touch-down.
    • Thinking of you, sending: a rising texture over the 700 ms hold, completing with a single sharp-soft transient on release.
    • Thinking of you, receiving: one transient with a short decay — the tap on the shoulder.
    • Cards opening: nothing. It is a visual moment and a haptic would cheapen it.
    • Everything else: nothing. No selection ticks, no success thumps, no error buzz.

    9.4  Sound

    Ribbon makes no sound it wasn't asked to make. The only audio in the product is voice-note playback and, later, the audio Bible. No chimes, no whooshes, no fire crackle — an ambient loop would be the single fastest way to make this app feel like a meditation product.

    10The copy system

    Words are the cheapest place to lose this brand and the cheapest place to prove it.

    10.1  The rules

    • Short sentences. Second person. Active voice.
    • Say the true small thing instead of the big warm thing. “Ruth left you a note at verse 4” beats “Deepen your spiritual connection.”
    • No exclamation points, anywhere, including in the store listing.
    • Grace is expressed by the interface being unbothered, not by the interface reassuring you.
    • Never name the person who hasn't done a thing.
    • A control says exactly what happens. Close the book closes the book.

    10.4  Naming, in the stores

    11Accessibility

    A long-form reading app has no excuse. These are requirements, not enhancements.

    Type scaling
    Full Dynamic Type through AX5 on iOS and 200% font scale on Android, with the measure reflowing rather than truncating. Literata's optical size axis tracks the scale. Nothing in the reading surface uses a fixed point size.
    Contrast
    Ivory #F3F0E6 on #0B0B0A and chartreuse #D6E45C on #0B0B0A both clear AAA comfortably. The risks are the muted and faint tones and the eight inks at 24% wash — every ink must be verified against both grounds for the text under the wash, not the wash itself.
    Color is never alone
    Note type is carried by shape (dot vs ring), not color. Person identity in a gutter mark is carried by the accessibility label, since a 6 pt ink dot cannot be the only signal of who left a note.
    Screen readers
    The fire's label must obey Law 2: “The fire is steady.” Never “80 percent complete.” Verse-by-verse navigation with rotor/local context. Gutter marks announce as “Note from Ruth, verse 9, not yet found.” A stack announces by author and never by count — “Notes from Ruth and Jacob, verse 9” — because “three notes” is a number the reader never asked for, and Law 2's hardest leaks are the ones only a screen reader says out loud.
    Voice notes
    Transcripts are mandatory, not a setting. A voice-only note is unreadable to a deaf member and unfindable to everyone in six months.
    Motor
    Every gesture has a tap equivalent. Long-press duration is adjustable via the system setting. The closing drag is always duplicated by the Wave mark.
    Reduce motion
    The fire holds a state instead of flickering. Morphs become cross-fades. The card turn becomes a fade. The thinking-of-you fill becomes an instant state change with the haptic intact.
    Reduce transparency
    iOS: glass becomes an opaque raised surface at --raised, keeping the same geometry. Never a mid-grey fallback.

    12The three skins

    One product, three native accents. The object model, the copy, and every law above are identical; what changes is the material.

    Law 5 · Native chrome, Ribbon contentThe platform owns the way things move, morph, and respond to touch. Ribbon owns everything inside the reading measure, the fire, the ink, and every word. Where the two conflict, the platform wins on chrome and Ribbon wins on content — and there is no third category, so any argument about who wins is really an argument about which of the two a thing is.

    12.1  iOS — Liquid Glass, tinted to near-invisible

    Target: iOS 26+, SwiftUI. Stance: use the real system material so we inherit the specular edge, the refraction, the interactive response, and every accessibility fallback for free — then tint it so far toward the unlit ground that it reads as depth rather than as glass.

    The one place we knowingly go against AppleApple's guidance says tint should carry semantic meaning, not decoration. Ribbon tints for brand: a near-black warm tint at high strength on .regular. On a #0B0B0A ground with paper grain, what survives is the specular edge and a faint lensing of whatever chartreuse sits beneath — which is precisely the goal. This is a deliberate deviation and should be written down as one, because a future reviewer will read it as a mistake.

    Where glass appears — and where it never does

    Implementation notes

    • Wrap every group of glass elements in a single GlassEffectContainer — separate containers cause glass to sample glass, and the result reads as a smudge.
    • .clear is wrong for us: it requires a media-rich background plus a dimming layer plus bold foreground content. We have none of those. .regular throughout.
    • Honour accessibilityReduceTransparency by swapping to .identity and painting --raised underneath. Test this path as a first-class design, not a fallback.
    • Soften or hide the automatic scroll-edge effect on the reading view — there is no top bar for it to separate the text from, so it reads as an unexplained smudge at the top of a page.
    • Use matchedTransitionSource + .navigationTransition(.zoom) for ember → ember record. It is the one place the system's morph is exactly the right metaphor: the ember grows into its own record.
    • Motion split: glass may morph with system spring physics — it is a material behaving as a material. Everything Ribbon draws obeys §9.1. That line is defensible and it should be stated in the code review checklist.

    Platform surfaces

    • App icon: the Cesso R. — ivory R, chartreuse period, on #0B0B0A. Three of the four iOS 26 variants: default, dark, and tinted. No clear variant. A glass R. was drawn and cut: the brief bans glow, bevel and gradient behind the mark, and the tinted-to-near-invisible argument that earns glass its place on floating chrome does not reach an icon sitting on a stranger's home screen.
    • Widgets and Live Activity: S24.
    • Haptics: Core Haptics patterns, not UIImpactFeedbackGenerator presets — the thinking-of-you tap needs a custom envelope and the presets are all too eager.
    • Billing: StoreKit 2, room-level entitlement verified server-side so the non-paying member's device never needs a receipt.
    • Fonts: Literata and the Alegreya Sans family bundled. Cesso ships as outlined vector only — it is an Adobe Font and cannot be embedded as a webfont or an app font.

    12.2  Android — Material 3 Expressive

    Target: Android 16+ (API 36), Jetpack Compose, Material 3 Expressive. Stance: take the shape system and the motion physics, decline the color system.

    The deliberate deviation on AndroidRibbon opts out of dynamic color. Material You would repaint the app from the user's wallpaper, and Ribbon's entire visual argument is one accent on one ground. A Ribbon tinted lavender because someone's wallpaper is lavender is not Ribbon. Ship a fixed color scheme and set isDynamicColor = false explicitly rather than by omission, so nobody turns it on later thinking it was an oversight.
    M3 Expressive featureRibbon's use
    Shape system + morphingYes, heavily. The presence form's lozenge→panel morph and the fire→ember transition are exactly what shape morphing is for.
    Physics-based motionYes, damped. Use the spatial spring tokens with damping near critical, so motion is physical but never overshoots. This is the Android answer to the same tension iOS has.
    Docked / floating toolbarsOnly for the long-press toolbar. The way out stays the Wave mark, custom.
    Button groups, split buttons, FAB menuNo. A FAB on this app would be wrong — there is no primary create action floating over a list, there is a book.
    Loading indicatorNo. Nothing in Ribbon should be visibly loading (§8).
    Bottom navigationNo. No tabs on any platform.
    RippleReplaced with a soft state layer at low opacity. No bounded ripple over Scripture — a ripple across a verse is exactly the “interface” feeling Literata is there to prevent.
    Predictive backYes — the room peels in behind the closing book. This is one place Android's system gesture is better than what we'd design.
    Edge-to-edgeMandatory. Gesture nav assumed; three-button nav gets extra bottom inset so the Wave never sits under the bar.
    • Typography: M3E's emphasized type roles map to Literata (display, body-large in reading) and Alegreya Sans (everything else). Small caps come from Alegreya Sans SC as a real face, not a textTransform.
    • Haptics: VibrationEffect.Composition for the thinking-of-you tap; the constants are too blunt.
    • Widgets: Glance, same content as iOS.
    • Billing: Play Billing, room-level entitlement, same server verification.

    12.3  Web — custom, and bookish

    Job: marketing and a real reading surface on one domain. Stance: this is the one platform with no native language to honour, so it goes furthest toward the printed book — which is also what makes it the right place for the hesitant partner to land from an invite link without installing anything (S16).

    The reading page

    • Single column, ~68 characters, centred, with real margins. The running head sits fixed at the top in small caps at low opacity — on desktop that is bookish rather than chrome, and it is the one place the mobile “no top bar” rule relaxes.
    • No position indicator of Ribbon's own. A drawn fore-edge — a page block at the right, showing roughly how far through you are — was designed and cut. A graphic whose only job is to report how much is left is a progress bar with the numerals removed, and it would have put a non-person element on the edge that belongs to people. The browser's own scrollbar stays, because it is the operating system's chrome and not Ribbon's report on you. Beyond that, position is shown by the text.
    • Presence keeps the right edge, as a narrow persistent rail rather than an emerging form, because a desktop window has the width to spare and nothing to gain from hiding it.
    • The gutter keeps the left edge, with room for the marks to sit further out than on a phone.

    13Data, sync, privacy

    Sync model
    Notes, highlights, cards and positions are last-write-wins per object, which is safe because objects are single-author. Highlights on the same range by different people are separate objects and never conflict.
    Presence
    The live signal is ephemeral and socket-only. Three things behind it do persist, and naming them exactly is the point: a rolling fuel window — who fed the fire within roughly the last 36 hours, discarded after; one last-read stamp per person per room, which renders as this morning and never as a clock time; and quiet-day marks. Nothing older than that is kept anywhere. The cap is what makes a consistency report impossible — not the absence of data, but the absence of history.
    Offline
    Downloaded Scripture is local and complete. Notes queue with local IDs and reconcile on reconnect. Voice audio uploads on Wi-Fi by default.
    Voice notes
    Stored encrypted; transcripts generated server-side at launch. On-device transcription is the better privacy answer and is an open question on cost and quality (§16).
    What is never collected
    Reading duration. Session counts. Wall-clock times. Any per-person reading record older than the rolling window above. Anything that could be assembled into a picture of a person's devotional consistency — including in analytics, including aggregated. If the data doesn't exist, nobody can be asked for it and no future product manager can build a streak from it.
    Analytics
    Product analytics limited to anonymous funnel events with no per-person reading history. No third-party SDK gets note content, verse addresses, or portraits.
    Export
    Any member can export their room's whole shelf at any time, as a readable file, without asking anyone. Closing a room triggers it automatically for everyone.

    14Money

    14.1  The model

    • ~$8 a month covers a room of two. One person pays; the room is entitled. The other never sees a paywall and never learns who pays.
    • Rooms of three to six: a flat monthly per room, priced above the pair.
    • Youth ministry: per leader, for N rooms, invoiced.
    • The first book is free, all the way through. Not a 7-day trial — a trial makes the clock the thing you're aware of, and a clock is a count. You read a whole book together, you get the ember, and the ask lands on the shelf, after the value.

    14.2  Where the ask appears

    Exactly two places: the shelf, after the first ember, and Plan (S22). Never in the reading surface, never at passage end, never after finishing a chapter, never as an interstitial, never as a badge.

    14.3  Lapsing

    A lapsed room is paused: presence stops, new notes stop. Reading, every existing note, every highlight and the whole shelf stay, forever. Scripture is never locked (§2.5). The copy names nobody:

    The room is paused. It can be started again any time.

    No countdown, no “you'll lose your data in 30 days,” no retention offer at cancellation, no dark pattern anywhere in the flow. The product's whole claim is patience; the billing flow is where that claim is actually tested.

    17The review checklist

    Run every screen past these before it ships. In this order.

    1. Is there a number anywhere near a handiwork? Count, fraction, percentage, day tally, streak, “x of y.” In the UI, in a widget, in a notification, in a screenshot, in an accessibility label. If yes, it does not ship.
    2. Could a person feel accused by anything on this screen? Including by absence, by an empty state, by a name attached to something not done.
    3. Would a person unsure about the Bible still be comfortable with this on their lock screen?
    4. Does the fire feel warm and unhurried, rather than gamified?
    5. Does it hold at the largest type size, with six people, on the longest chapter?
    6. Is anything from the never-ship list present? A cross in the mark, a cartoon flame, a gradient hero, glass over content, confetti, badges, trophies, a numeric icon badge, a church-bulletin layout.
    7. Does every string pass the voice rules? No exclamation points. No apologies. Nobody named for something undone.
    8. Is it obviously made by a person rather than assembled from platform defaults?
    The last wordEverything in this document is downstream of one sentence: a room, not a program. When a decision here turns out to be wrong — and some will — replace it with whatever better serves that sentence, and leave the reasoning behind for whoever comes next.
